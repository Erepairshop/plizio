Weser River & Bremen Port

View on the map

The Weser is Bremen's lifeline, connecting the city to the North Sea. Bremen's port was for centuries a central hub for trade between Europe and overseas.

Facts

  • Weser: 452 km long, flows into the North Sea.
  • Bremen was a Hanseatic city with major maritime trade.
  • Bremerhaven is located at the Weser estuary.

Bremen

Bremen is a historic Hanseatic city on the Weser River in northwestern Germany. Together with Bremerhaven, it forms the smallest German federal state. The city is world-famous for the fairy tale of the Town Musicians of Bremen and its maritime trading tradition. The historic City Hall and the Roland statue on the market square are UNESCO World Heritage sites, testifying to the city's proud citizenry.

Bremen Roland & Town Hall

Bremen's Roland is a 10-meter knight statue on the market square, standing since 1404 as a symbol of freedom and market rights. Together with the Gothic Town Hall, it was declared a UNESCO World Heritage Site in 2004.

Schnoor Quarter

The Schnoor quarter is Bremen's oldest district, with small fishermen's houses from the 15th century. The narrow alleys and historic half-timbered houses make it one of the best-preserved medieval quarters in northern Germany.
