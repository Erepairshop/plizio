Leipzig – Books & Cars

View on the map

Leipzig is traditionally a book city with Germany's oldest book fair. Today BMW and Porsche have established themselves here, making the city a modern industrial location.

Facts

  • Germany's oldest book fair (since 16th c.).
  • BMW and Porsche operate modern plants here.
  • Important logistics and commercial center.

Dresden

Dresden, the capital of Saxony, is often referred to as the 'Florence on the Elbe' due to its magnificent Baroque architecture and its location in the Elbe Valley. The city suffered devastating destruction in February 1945, but historical masterpieces like the Frauenkirche were masterfully reconstructed. Dresden is a cultural center of European standing, characterized by art collections, operas, and music festivals. At the same time, it is a modern technology hub in Silicon Saxony today.

Leipzig

Leipzig is the most populous city in Saxony and an important historical center for trade and culture. It is often called the city of music, as Johann Sebastian Bach worked here for a long time as the cantor of St. Thomas Church. Furthermore, Leipzig played a key role in the Peaceful Revolution of 1989, which led to the fall of the Berlin Wall. Today, the city is a vibrant university and trade fair center.

Fichtelberg

The Fichtelberg in the Ore Mountains (Erzgebirge) is the highest mountain in the state of Saxony and an important symbol of the region. The surrounding Ore Mountains on the border with the Czech Republic were historically one of the richest mining regions in Europe and are world-renowned for their wooden art, particularly Schwibbögen (candle arches), nutcrackers, and smoking figures. The mining landscape is today a UNESCO World Heritage site. In winter, the Fichtelberg and the resort town of Oberwiesenthal turn into the most important Saxon winter sports center.

Zwinger

The Dresden Zwinger is a Baroque palace ensemble in Dresden, built by Augustus the Strong in the early 18th century. The Zwinger today houses several important museums, including the Old Masters Picture Gallery with Raphael's Sistine Madonna.

Leipzig Monday demonstrations

The Leipzig Monday demonstrations were weekly protests against the East German regime in autumn 1989, leading to the Peaceful Revolution and the fall of the Berlin Wall. The rally on 9 October 1989 with 70,000 people is considered a turning point in German history.
