Lübeck Holstentor

View on the map

The Holstentor is the landmark of Lübeck and one of Germany's most famous medieval city gates.

The Holstentor (Holstein Gate) in Lübeck is the most iconic city gate in Germany and a global symbol of Hanseatic power. Built between 1464 and 1478, this monumental red-brick gate served as both a defensive bastion and a prestigious entrance to the 'Queen of the Hanseatic League.' Its two massive round towers and its characteristic lean – caused by the soft marshy ground – create a unique and imposing silhouette. The gate's inscription, 'CONCORDIA DOMI FORIS PAX' (Harmony at home, peace abroad), reflects the ideals of the wealthy merchant city. Today, the Holstentor houses a museum dedicated to Lübeck's maritime trade history and remains the definitive landmark of Northern Germany's UNESCO heritage. Facts

  • The Holstentor was built in 1478.
  • Until 1975 it appeared on the 50-Mark banknote.
  • The gate leans noticeably to the side because it was built on soft ground.
  • UNESCO World Heritage site and Germany's most famous city gate.
  • Built in the late 15th century to defend the western entrance of Lübeck.
  • Famous for its significant tilt and sinking into the soft soil.
  • Features walls up to 3.5 meters thick to withstand cannon fire.
  • The gate was featured on the 50 DM banknote and the 2 Euro coin.
  • Houses a museum exploring the 'Power of Trade' in the Middle Ages.

Lübeck

Lübeck, often referred to as the 'Queen of the Hanseatic League,' is a historic port city in Schleswig-Holstein. Its medieval old town is situated on an island enclosed by the Trave River and is a UNESCO World Heritage site. The silhouette is dominated by seven distinctive church towers, earning it the nickname City of the Seven Towers. Lübeck is renowned for its outstanding Brick Gothic ensemble, which includes the world-famous Holsten Gate. Additionally, the city is the home of the world-renowned Lübeck Marzipan and is inseparably linked to Nobel laureates Thomas Mann, Günter Grass, and Willy Brandt.
