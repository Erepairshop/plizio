Nordertor Flensburg

View on the map

The Nordertor is a medieval city gate and the 15th-century landmark of Flensburg.

Facts

  • The Nordertor was built around 1595.
  • It is the only surviving city gate of Flensburg.
  • The gate is located at the northern end of the old town.

Kiel

Kiel is the state capital of Schleswig-Holstein and a major port city on the Baltic Sea. Its maritime DNA shapes the entire cityscape: Kiel is located at the entrance to the Kiel Canal, the busiest artificial waterway in the world. The city is internationally famous for the 'Kiel Week' (Kieler Woche), the world's largest sailing event and a massive summer festival. Economically, shipbuilding, marine technology, and ferry traffic to Scandinavia are highly prominent. As a university city, Kiel offers a young, vibrant atmosphere, surrounded by beaches and fjord landscapes.

Lübeck

Lübeck, often referred to as the 'Queen of the Hanseatic League,' is a historic port city in Schleswig-Holstein. Its medieval old town is situated on an island enclosed by the Trave River and is a UNESCO World Heritage site. The silhouette is dominated by seven distinctive church towers, earning it the nickname City of the Seven Towers. Lübeck is renowned for its outstanding Brick Gothic ensemble, which includes the world-famous Holsten Gate. Additionally, the city is the home of the world-renowned Lübeck Marzipan and is inseparably linked to Nobel laureates Thomas Mann, Günter Grass, and Willy Brandt.

Sylt

Sylt is the largest North Frisian island and, as a place of longing in the North Sea, attracts millions of guests. The island offers roaring surf with miles of beaches on the west side, while the east side borders the calm Wadden Sea. Characteristic are thatched-roof houses, exclusive gastronomy, and picturesque dune landscapes. Coastal protection is essential here, as Sylt constantly loses land during storms.
