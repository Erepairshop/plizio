Rhön (Hesse)

View on the map

The Rhön is a low mountain range at the tripoint of Bavaria, Hesse, and Thuringia, known for its wide basalt landscapes.

The Rhön is a stunning mountain range shared by Hesse, Bavaria, and Thuringia, famously known as the 'Land of Open Distances.' Unlike other densely forested German mountains, the Rhön is characterized by vast, treeless plateaus and volcanic peaks that offer breathtaking panoramic views. The Wasserkuppe (950 meters) is the highest peak in Hesse and the cradle of modern gliding, where the first unpowered flights were performed in the early 20th century. Today, the Rhön is a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve, dedicated to sustainable agriculture and the protection of rare ecosystems like the high moors. It is a haven for hikers, star-gazers (due to its low light pollution), and those seeking the quiet beauty of a unique, wind-swept landscape.   • Highest peak in the Hessian part is the Wasserkuppe at 950 meters.
  • Home to the 'Rotes Moor', one of the largest high moors in Hesse.
  • A certified 'International Dark Sky Park' for stargazing.
  • Spans three German states: Hesse, Bavaria, and Thuringia.

Related places

Wasserkuppe

At 950 meters, the Wasserkuppe is the highest mountain in the Rhön Mountains and the highest elevation in the state of Hesse. Historically, it is considered the 'cradle of gliding,' as aviation pioneers conducted the first unmotorized flights here in the 1920s. Even today, the mountain is a mecca for glider, paraglider, and model aircraft pilots. The treeless summit offers expansive views over the low mountain landscape. In addition to aviation sports, the Wasserkuppe is a popular recreation area featuring a summer toboggan run, ski lifts, and the German Gliding Museum.

Großer Feldberg

The Großer Feldberg at 879 m is the highest peak of the Taunus mountains and of Hesse (excluding Rhön) and lies close to Frankfurt am Main. On clear days the Alps can be seen from its summit, and it hosts numerous transmission facilities.

Wiesbaden

Wiesbaden is the state capital of Hesse and one of the oldest spa towns in Europe. Famous for its hot thermal springs, the city attracted aristocrats and artists from all over the world in the 19th century, earning it the nickname 'Nice of the North.' The city's architecture is strongly characterized by Historicism and Classicism, as it was largely spared from destruction during World War II. Today, Wiesbaden is an important service center, home to the Federal Criminal Police Office and the Federal Statistical Office. The elegant Wilhelmstraße and the magnificent Kurhaus underline its sophisticated flair.

Frankfurt am Main

Frankfurt am Main is the financial center of Germany and a major transportation hub in Europe. The city impresses with its distinctive skyline, which earned it the nickname 'Mainhattan'. Historically, Frankfurt was an important site for the election and coronation of German emperors for centuries. Today, it is an ultra-modern, international metropolis and home to the European Central Bank.
