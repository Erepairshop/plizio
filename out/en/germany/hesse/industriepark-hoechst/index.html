Industriepark Höchst

View on the map

One of Europe's largest chemical and pharmaceutical sites, located on the outskirts of Frankfurt.

Industriepark Höchst, located on the banks of the Main in Frankfurt, is one of the largest and most successful chemical and pharmaceutical research and production sites in Europe. Originally the headquarters of the legendary Hoechst AG, the park has evolved into a multi-company site hosting over 90 firms, including global giants like Sanofi and Bayer. The park functions as a 'city within a city,' with its own power plants, fire department, and waste management systems. It is a powerhouse of innovation, focusing on life sciences, specialty chemicals, and biotechnology. With over 22,000 employees and billions in annual investment, Industriepark Höchst is a critical driver of the German economy and a center for developing the medicines and materials of tomorrow. Wiesbaden

Wiesbaden is the state capital of Hesse and one of the oldest spa towns in Europe. Famous for its hot thermal springs, the city attracted aristocrats and artists from all over the world in the 19th century, earning it the nickname 'Nice of the North.' The city's architecture is strongly characterized by Historicism and Classicism, as it was largely spared from destruction during World War II. Today, Wiesbaden is an important service center, home to the Federal Criminal Police Office and the Federal Statistical Office. The elegant Wilhelmstraße and the magnificent Kurhaus underline its sophisticated flair.

Kassel

Kassel is located in northern Hesse and is a city globally renowned for its art, culture, and historic parks. Every five years, the city hosts the documenta, one of the world's most significant exhibitions for contemporary art. The Bergpark Wilhelmshöhe, Europe's largest hillside park, is a UNESCO World Heritage site and impresses with its monumental water features and the massive Hercules statue. Kassel is also closely connected to the lives and work of the Brothers Grimm, who collected the majority of their world-famous fairy tales here.

Wasserkuppe

At 950 meters, the Wasserkuppe is the highest mountain in the Rhön Mountains and the highest elevation in the state of Hesse. Historically, it is considered the 'cradle of gliding,' as aviation pioneers conducted the first unmotorized flights here in the 1920s. Even today, the mountain is a mecca for glider, paraglider, and model aircraft pilots. The treeless summit offers expansive views over the low mountain landscape. In addition to aviation sports, the Wasserkuppe is a popular recreation area featuring a summer toboggan run, ski lifts, and the German Gliding Museum.
