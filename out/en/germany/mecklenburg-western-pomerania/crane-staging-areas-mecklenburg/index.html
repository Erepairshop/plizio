Crane Staging Areas Mecklenburg

View on the map

Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania is Europe's most important transit area for grey cranes heading to their wintering grounds.

The 'Crane Paradise' of Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania is one of Europe's most spectacular wildlife spectacles, occurring every spring and autumn. The region around the Darss-Zingst lagoon and the island of Rügen serves as a vital resting and feeding ground for up to 100,000 Eurasian cranes during their migration between Scandinavia and the south. These 'birds of luck' are celebrated for their haunting calls and dramatic dancing displays in the shallow waters of the lagoons (Bodden). The National Park and specialized observation centers like 'Kranorama' provide world-class opportunities for visitors to witness this natural wonder without disturbing the sensitive birds. It is a powerful symbol of the region's role as a key link in the global flyway and its commitment to international species protection. Sachkunde K3 – Bird migration and habitat protection.

Facts

  • Over 60,000 cranes rest in the region each year.
  • The crane migration season lasts from October to November.
  • Nearby Lake Linum is one of the largest crane roosting sites in Europe.
  • Up to 100,000 cranes gather in the region during peak migration.
  • The 'Kranoram' near Günz is a specialized barrier-free observation post.
  • Cranes are known as 'Birds of Luck' in German folklore.
  • The region is the most important crane stopover in Central Europe.
  • Autumn migration peaks in October, attracting thousands of nature lovers.
  • Cranes feed in the local corn fields and sleep in the shallow lagoons.

Schwerin

Schwerin is the state capital of Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania and the smallest German state capital by population. It is picturesquely embedded in a landscape of lakes, often referred to as the 'City of Seven Lakes.' Its outstanding landmark is Schwerin Castle, which rests majestically on its own island in Lake Schwerin and is considered the Neuschwanstein of the North. The historic old town fascinates visitors with well-preserved half-timbered houses and the Schwerin Cathedral. Despite its small size, Schwerin offers a rich cultural tradition with significant museums and the State Theatre.

Rostock

Rostock is the largest city in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania and is picturesquely situated on the Warnow River, close to the Baltic Sea coast. The city has a rich past as a powerful member of the Hanseatic League, reflected in the magnificent merchant houses and imposing brick churches of the old town. The University of Rostock, founded in 1419, is one of the oldest in Northern Europe, giving the city a dynamic, student-friendly flair. The Warnemünde district attracts tourists with its wide sandy beaches and historic lighthouse. Economically, port management, cruise tourism, and innovative technologies are also of major importance.

Müritz

Lake Müritz is Germany's largest wholly inland lake (excluding Bodensee) and is located in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania. It is the centerpiece of Müritz National Park and an important bird sanctuary where cranes and white-tailed eagles rest.

Lake Schwerin lies in and around the state capital Schwerin in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania and is one of the state's largest lakes. The magnificent Schwerin Castle sits on an island in the lake, giving the city's skyline a fairy-tale appearance.

Rügen

Rügen is Germany's largest island by area and is located in the Baltic Sea. It is most famous for its striking, brilliant white chalk cliffs in the Jasmund National Park, which the painter Caspar David Friedrich immortalized. Rügen also offers fine sandy beaches, elegant Baltic Sea resorts with classical resort architecture, and untouched nature. It is a traditional and popular holiday destination.
