Rostock – Baltic Sea Port

View on the map

Rostock is the largest port on the German Baltic Sea coast. Ferry connections go to Denmark, Sweden and Finland – Rostock is the gateway to the Baltic region.

Facts

  • Germany's largest Baltic Sea port.
  • Ferries to Denmark, Sweden, Finland.
  • Significant for grain and raw material handling.

Schwerin

Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania's capital lies on Lake Schwerin and is famous for the magnificent Schwerin Castle, which sits on an island in the lake. The city is among the oldest towns in northern Germany.

Rostock

Rostock is Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania's largest city and an important Baltic Sea port with a traditionally rich university founded in 1419. The city is a popular excursion destination thanks to the Baltic resort of Warnemünde right next to the city.

Müritz

Lake Müritz is Germany's largest wholly inland lake (excluding Bodensee) and is located in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania. It is the centerpiece of Müritz National Park and an important bird sanctuary where cranes and white-tailed eagles rest.

Schweriner See

Lake Schwerin lies in and around the state capital Schwerin in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania and is one of the state's largest lakes. The magnificent Schwerin Castle sits on an island in the lake, giving the city's skyline a fairy-tale appearance.

Rügen

Rügen is Germany's largest island in the Baltic Sea and lies in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania. It is known for the white chalk cliffs of Jasmund National Park, picturesque seaside resorts like Binz and Sellin, and the Rügen Causeway connecting it to the mainland.

Usedom

Usedom is a Baltic Sea island in Mecklenburg-Western Pomerania shared between Germany and Poland. It is known for its long sandy beaches, the Kaiserbäder spa towns Ahlbeck, Heringsdorf, and Bansin, and historic spa architecture villas.
