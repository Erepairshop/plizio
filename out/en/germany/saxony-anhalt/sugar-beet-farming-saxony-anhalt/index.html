Sugar Beet Farming (Saxony-Anhalt)

View on the map

Saxony-Anhalt is one of Germany's most important sugar beet growing regions. The fertile soils of the Magdeburg Borde and the Saale-Unstrut region are ideal for cultivating this crop. The sugar industry has a long tradition in the region.

The Magdeburg Fertile Plain (Magdeburger Börde) is the heart of German sugar beet production, famous for its exceptionally fertile black soil (Schwarzerde). Since the early 19th century, the region has been a powerhouse of the sugar industry, following the development of the sugar extraction process from beets by Franz Carl Achard. This 'white gold' transformed the regional economy, leading to the construction of massive sugar factories and the growth of wealthy agricultural towns. Today, Saxony-Anhalt remains a leader in sustainable sugar beet cultivation and processing, producing millions of tons of sugar for the European market. The sugar beet is a symbol of the region's agricultural strength and its historic role in the industrialization of food. Sachkunde K4 – Agriculture and the history of sugar production.

Facts

  • The Magdeburg Borde is one of Germany's most fertile regions.
  • Saxony-Anhalt is one of the largest sugar beet producers.
  • Sugar beets are processed into sugar and bioethanol.
  • Harvesting takes place in autumn.
  • Home to the most fertile soil in Germany (Magdeburger Börde).
  • A global center for sugar beet production for over 200 years.
  • Site of the first industrial sugar beet factory in the world (Klein Wanzleben).
  • The 'Börde' soil received the maximum score of 100 on the German soil scale.
  • A major hub for the modern European sugar and bioethanol industry.
  • Historically the foundation of the regional wealth in the 19th century.

Magdeburg

Magdeburg, the state capital of Saxony-Anhalt, looks back on an eventful history spanning over 1,200 years. Once, it was one of the most important metropolises in the Holy Roman Empire and the favorite palace of Emperor Otto the Great. The city on the Elbe experienced multiple destructions, including during the Thirty Years' War, but always rose anew. Today, Magdeburg combines historical buildings like the imposing Cathedral with modern architecture such as the Hundertwasser house 'Green Citadel'.

Brocken

At 1,141 meters, the Brocken is the highest peak in the Harz mountain range and in all of Northern Germany. Located in Saxony-Anhalt, the mountain features alpine vegetation due to its extremely harsh microclimate, which is typically only found at much higher altitudes. The summit is shrouded in fog for almost 300 days a year, inspiring numerous myths and legends, especially those of witches dancing here on Walpurgis Night. Goethe immortalized the Brocken in his masterpiece 'Faust.' Today, the summit is a popular tourist destination that can be comfortably reached via the historic steam-powered Brocken Railway.

Harz

The Harz is the northernmost low mountain range in Germany and a place full of myths, deep forests, and steep cliffs. The region spans three federal states and was an important center for silver and ore mining for centuries, the traces of which are still visible today. The highest peak is the legendary Brocken, often described in literature as the meeting place of witches on Walpurgis Night. Nostalgic narrow-gauge railways pulled by steam locomotives transport tourists through this rugged, impressive landscape.

Lutherstadt Eisleben is the birthplace and death city of Martin Luther and is located in Saxony-Anhalt. The city with Luther's birth house and death house, as well as the baptismal church St. Peter and Paul, has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1996.

Quedlinburg – Collegiate Church

Quedlinburg, on the northern edge of the Harz Mountains in Saxony-Anhalt, is a true architectural treasure trove. The town impresses with a unique ensemble of over 2,000 lovingly restored half-timbered houses spanning eight centuries. Perched on a prominent sandstone rock are the Romanesque collegiate church and the Renaissance castle, which once formed the center of the Ottonian ruling dynasty. Since 1994, this invaluable historical heritage has been entirely part of the UNESCO World Heritage.
