Naumburg Cathedral

View on the map

Naumburg Cathedral is a Romanesque-Gothic structure from the 13th century and has been a UNESCO World Heritage site since 2018. Famous are the founder figures in the west choir, including the legendary Uta von Naumburg. The artwork is considered one of the most significant testimonies of medieval sculpture.

Naumburg Cathedral, dedicated to St. Peter and St. Paul, is one of the most significant architectural monuments of the European Middle Ages and a UNESCO World Heritage site since 2018. It is world-famous for the 'Naumbburg Master,' an anonymous sculptor who created the twelve donor statues in the west choir, including the iconic 'Uta von Ballenstedt.' These statues are considered masterpieces of realism and psychological depth, unlike anything else in 13th-century art. The cathedral uniquely features two choirs, reflecting the transition from Romanesque to Gothic architecture. Perched in the heart of the Saale-Unstrut wine region, it remains a majestic symbol of the spiritual and artistic wealth of the medieval Holy Roman Empire. Quedlinburg – Collegiate Church

Quedlinburg, on the northern edge of the Harz Mountains in Saxony-Anhalt, is a true architectural treasure trove. The town impresses with a unique ensemble of over 2,000 lovingly restored half-timbered houses spanning eight centuries. Perched on a prominent sandstone rock are the Romanesque collegiate church and the Renaissance castle, which once formed the center of the Ottonian ruling dynasty. Since 1994, this invaluable historical heritage has been entirely part of the UNESCO World Heritage.
