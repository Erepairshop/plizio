Chiemsee

View on the map

Lake Chiemsee is Bavaria's largest lake and is often called the 'Bavarian Sea'. On its islands Herreninsel and Fraueninsel lie historic monasteries and the magnificent Herrenchiemsee Palace, which King Ludwig II had built modeled on Versailles.

Facts

  • Bavaria's largest lake.
  • Herrenchiemsee: Palace of King Ludwig II.
  • Fraueninsel: ancient monastery.

Starnberger See

Lake Starnberg lies about 25 km southwest of Munich in Bavaria and is one of the largest lakes in the German Alpine foothills. It is a popular recreation area for Munich residents and known for the mysterious circumstances of King Ludwig II's death in 1886.

Ammersee

Lake Ammersee lies in western Upper Bavaria southwest of Munich and at 47 km² is Bavaria's third-largest lake after Chiemsee and Starnberger See. The Andechs Monastery on the Holy Mountain near the Ammersee is a famous pilgrimage destination and renowned brewery monastery.

München

Munich, the state capital of Bavaria, combines Bavarian tradition with modern technology and cosmopolitanism. Located on the Isar River in southern Germany, the city is an economic powerhouse. Historical architecture dominates the old town around Marienplatz. At the same time, Munich is a globally significant location for the automotive industry, media, and research.

Nürnberg

Nuremberg is the second largest city in Bavaria and the historical center of Franconia. The city looks back on a glorious past in the Holy Roman Empire, evidenced by the imposing Imperial Castle above the old town. In the 20th century, Nuremberg became world-famous for the Nazi party rallies and later the Nuremberg Trials. Today, the Christkindlesmarkt, one of the world's most famous Christmas markets, is a major attraction.
