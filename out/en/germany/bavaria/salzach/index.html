Salzach

View on the map

The Salzach is a right tributary of the Inn, flowing through the Berchtesgaden Land and the border region between Bavaria and Austria.

The Salzach is a powerful alpine river that has shaped the history and geography of the Austro-Bavarian border for millennia. Its name, meaning 'Salt River,' refers to the vital role it played in transporting the 'white gold' from the mines of Hallein and Berchtesgaden to the markets of Europe. As the largest tributary of the Inn, it flows through spectacular gorges and historic towns like Burghausen, which boasts the world's longest castle. Today, the Salzach is a popular destination for cycling and hiking, and its restoration projects have made it a model for river conservation in Central Europe. It remains a living boundary between nations and a vital artery of the Eastern Alps. Földrajz K7 – Alpine rivers and historical trade routes.

Facts

  • Total length approx. 225 km, partly forming the Bavarian-Austrian border.
  • Rises in the Austrian state of Salzburg.
  • Flows into the Inn at Braunau am Inn.
  • An important waterway for the historic salt trade.
  • Part of the Danube river basin.
  • The largest and most important tributary of the Inn river.
  • Served as the primary transport route for salt for centuries.
  • Flows past the world's longest castle in the town of Burghausen.
  • The river is approximately 225 kilometers long.
  • Forms the natural border between Germany and Austria for 59 km.
  • Famous for its dramatic 'Salzachöfen' gorge near Golling.

Regensburg

Regensburg is located in eastern Bavaria at the northernmost point of the Danube River and is considered one of Germany's best-preserved medieval cities. Its history dates back to the Romans, who built the Castra Regina camp here. The historic center, featuring the famous Stone Bridge and the Gothic St. Peter's Cathedral, is a UNESCO World Heritage site. The old town, characterized by narrow alleys, patrician towers, and an Italian flair, transports visitors back in time. Today, Regensburg is a lively university city with a thriving economy driven by the automotive and electronics industries.

Augsburg

Augsburg is one of Germany's oldest cities, founded by the Romans, and is located in southwestern Bavaria. Historically, the city gained immense significance through the Fugger and Welser merchant families, who transformed Augsburg into a global financial hub during the Renaissance. Testaments to this wealth include the magnificent City Hall and the Fuggerei, the world's oldest social housing complex. Due to its sophisticated historic water management system, the city was designated a UNESCO World Heritage site. Today, Augsburg blends its rich Roman and medieval heritage with a dynamic economy and a lively university.

Bayreuth

Bayreuth is an independent city in Upper Franconia (Bavaria) and is world-famous for its annual Richard Wagner Festival. The city breathes music and culture; Wagner had the unique Festspielhaus built here on the Green Hill specifically for the performance of his operas. Aside from Wagner, the cityscape was decisively shaped in the 18th century by Margravine Wilhelmine, who commissioned the Margravial Opera House, now a UNESCO World Heritage site. Numerous palaces, expansive parks like the Hermitage, and a traditional brewing culture make Bayreuth a magnet for cultural tourists from around the world.
