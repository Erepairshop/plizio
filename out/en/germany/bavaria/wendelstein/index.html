Wendelstein

View on the map

The Wendelstein is a prominent peak in the Bavarian Pre-Alps and one of the most popular excursion destinations in Bavaria.

The Wendelstein is one of the most prominent and beloved peaks in the Bavarian Alps, standing at 1,838 meters. It is famous for its spectacular 360-degree views that stretch from the peaks of the Central Alps to the Bavarian foothills. Since 1912, the summit has been accessible via Germany's oldest rack railway, a masterpiece of alpine engineering that still operates today alongside a modern cable car. The summit is also a center for science, housing an observatory and a high-altitude meteorological station. Visitors can explore Germany's highest church and a fascinating ice cave, making the Wendelstein a unique destination where nature, technology, and spirituality meet high above the clouds. Facts

  • Summit elevation is 1,838 metres above sea level.
  • Since 1912 the Wendelstein has been accessible by rack railway.
  • An observatory and a weather station are located on the summit.
  • On clear days it offers panoramic views of the Alps.
  • Popular for hiking and ski tours.
  • Summit height of 1,838 meters in the Mangfall Mountains.
  • Home to Germany's oldest rack railway, opened in 1912.
  • Features Germany's highest church, the Wendelsteinkirchlein.
  • Hosts a world-class solar and astronomical observatory at the top.
  • Includes a natural ice cave located at 1,711 meters altitude.
  • Offers views of over 200 Alpine peaks on a clear day.

Zugspitze

The Zugspitze is Germany's highest mountain at 2,962 meters and belongs to the Wetterstein Mountains in the Alps. The state border between Germany and Austria runs across its summit. Three cable cars provide access to the mountain massif, offering tourists an unparalleled 360-degree panoramic view of hundreds of Alpine peaks. Thanks to the glacier and excellent infrastructure, the Zugspitze is a paradise for skiers and alpinists.

Großer Arber

At 1,456 meters, the Großer Arber is the highest mountain of the Bohemian Forest and the Bavarian Forest, earning it the nickname 'King of the Bavarian Forest.' The mountain is located in Lower Bavaria near the Czech border and is an outstanding natural monument. Its striking silhouette is formed by four rocky ridges, and the summit lies above the tree line. The mountain is the most important ski area in the Bavarian Forest and has repeatedly hosted Alpine Ski World Cup races. Numerous hiking trails, the picturesque Großer Arbersee (Lake Arber), and dense forests characterize the region.

Watzmann

At 2,713 meters, the Watzmann is the central mountain massif of the Berchtesgaden Alps in Bavaria and the third-highest mountain in Germany. It is famous for its unique shape, which according to legend represents a petrified royal family (King Watzmann, his wife, and their children). The massive Watzmann East Face (Ostwand) is the highest rock face in the Eastern Alps and presents an extreme challenge for alpinists. The mountain towers majestically over the deep blue Königssee (Lake Königs) and is the iconic landmark of the Berchtesgaden National Park. It attracts thousands of mountaineers and hikers annually.

Nürnberg

Nuremberg is the second largest city in Bavaria and the historical center of Franconia.
