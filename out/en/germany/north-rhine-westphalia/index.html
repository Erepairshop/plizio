Regions

North Rhine-Westphalia

North Rhine-Westphalia is the most populous state in Germany and the economic powerhouse of the republic. Historically shaped by coal and steel mining in the Ruhr Area, the region has undergone a massive structural shift towards services, technology, and education. Cities like Cologne with its famous Cathedral and the state capital Düsseldorf on the Rhine are cultural magnets. The state offers an immense density of museums, theaters, and universities. In addition to the densely populated areas, there are also large forests and natural reserves such as the Sauerland and the Teutoburg Forest.

With around 18 million residents, it is the most populous German state.The Ruhr Area was historically the largest industrial center in Europe.The Cologne Cathedral is one of the most visited tourist landmarks in Germany.The state capital, Düsseldorf, is a prominent center for fashion and the arts.

Cities

Düsseldorf

Düsseldorf, the state capital of North Rhine-Westphalia, is a center for fashion, art, and business on the Rhine River. The city is famous for the Königsallee, one of Europe's most exclusive shopping streets, and its vibrant Rhine promenade. As a financial center and major trade fair location, Düsseldorf has international appeal. At the same time, the city retains its local charm with its historic Old Town, known as the 'longest bar in the world'.

Köln

Cologne is the fourth-largest city in Germany, located on the Rhine River in North Rhine-Westphalia. The city looks back on a history of over 2000 years, dating back to Roman times. Cologne is globally renowned for its imposing Cathedral and the exuberant Rhenish Carnival. Today, the Rhine metropolis is a cosmopolitan cultural center and an important location for media and art.

Aachen

Aachen is the westernmost major city in Germany, located in the border triangle with the Netherlands and Belgium. The historical significance of the city is enormous, as it was the preferred residence of Emperor Charlemagne, whose tomb is located in the impressive Aachen Cathedral. Due to its hot thermal springs, Aachen is a traditional spa town. The local specialty, the Aachener Printen (gingerbread), is popular far beyond the borders.

Bonn

Bonn is located in the south of North Rhine-Westphalia on the banks of the Rhine and has a history spanning over 2,000 years. The city is known worldwide as the birthplace of the famous composer Ludwig van Beethoven. From 1949 until the reunification of Germany, Bonn served as the seat of government and provisional capital of the Federal Republic. Today, the city distinguishes itself as a UN location, headquarters for many international organizations, and a center for science and telecommunications.

Duisburg

Duisburg is located at the confluence of the Ruhr and Rhine rivers and heavily shapes the western Ruhr area through its waterfront location. The city is home to the world's largest inland port, a crucial hub in European logistics. Once purely a steel and coal center, Duisburg is successfully transforming today through projects like the Landschaftspark Duisburg-Nord. The historical industrial culture harmoniously blends with new leisure and cultural offerings.

Oberhausen

Oberhausen is located in the western Ruhr Area in North Rhine-Westphalia and stands as a symbol of the region's successful structural transformation. Once known as the 'Cradle of the Ruhr Industry,' iron and steel production dominated here for decades. Today, the 'Neue Mitte Oberhausen' (New Center) is the heart of the city, built on former industrial wastelands. It is home to CentrO, one of Europe's largest shopping and leisure centers. The Gasometer, a monumental former gas holder, now serves as a gigantic exhibition hall and is the city's highly visible landmark.

Gelsenkirchen

Gelsenkirchen, centrally located in the Ruhr Area, was once known as the 'City of 1000 Fires' when the winding towers and smoking chimneys of collieries and coking plants dominated its skyline. Following the end of the coal mining era, Gelsenkirchen underwent a massive transformation and now focuses on solar energy and green technologies. The Veltins-Arena is home to the legendary football club FC Schalke 04, which forms the beating heart of local identity. Cultural highlights like the Musiktheater im Revier and the Nordsternpark, built on a former colliery site, round off the picture of the modern city.

Bochum

Bochum is a major city in the heart of the Ruhr Area, embodying the successful transition from heavy industry to a knowledge and service-based society. In the 1960s, the first university in the Ruhr region, the Ruhr University Bochum (RUB), was founded here, marking the dawn of a new era. Culturally, the city is nationally renowned for its award-winning theater, Schauspielhaus Bochum, and the musical 'Starlight Express,' which has been running for decades. The German Mining Museum offers fascinating insights into the industrial past, both below and above ground.

Bielefeld

Bielefeld is the economic and cultural center of the Ostwestfalen-Lippe region in the northeast of North Rhine-Westphalia. The city is charmingly situated along the Teutoburg Forest and is overlooked by the historic Sparrenburg Castle, its most famous landmark. Originally a major linen and textile city, Bielefeld has today developed into a modern hub for mechanical engineering, food production, and services—known among others for the Dr. Oetker corporation. A long-running satirical internet conspiracy theory, the 'Bielefeld Conspiracy,' jokingly claims that the city doesn't actually exist.

History

Aachen Cathedral

Aachen Cathedral is Germany's oldest cathedral and was built by Charlemagne around 800 as a palace chapel. It was the coronation church of 32 German kings and emperors and in 1978 became Germany's first UNESCO World Heritage Site.

Bundeshaus Bonn

The Bundeshaus in Bonn was from 1949 to 1999 the seat of the German Bundestag and Bundesrat, making it the heart of West German democracy. After the move to Berlin in 1999, it is now home to Deutsche Welle and various federal authorities.

Paderborn Cathedral

Paderborn Cathedral is one of Germany's oldest cathedrals, dating back to a foundation by Charlemagne in 799 when he established a bishopric here. The present building dates from the 11th to 13th century and is considered an important testament to the Carolingian era.

Landmarks

Kölner Dom

Cologne Cathedral is a Roman Catholic church in Cologne and one of the largest Gothic cathedrals in the world. Construction began in 1248, but it was not completed until the 19th century. As home to the Shrine of the Three Kings, it has been an important pilgrimage site since the Middle Ages. Its striking twin towers define the Cologne skyline and make it Germany's most visited landmark.

Zollverein Coal Mine

The Zollverein Coal Mine in Essen was once the largest and highest-yielding coal mine in the world and was considered a masterpiece of industrial architecture. With its distinctive double headframe, it is now the most famous landmark of the Ruhr area. After its closure in 1986, the vast area was transformed into a cultural center. Since 2001, the entire complex has been a UNESCO World Heritage site, representing the transformation of an entire region.
