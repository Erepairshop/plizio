Gelsenkirchen

View on the map

Gelsenkirchen, centrally located in the Ruhr Area, was once known as the 'City of 1000 Fires' when the winding towers and smoking chimneys of collieries and coking plants dominated its skyline. Following the end of the coal mining era, Gelsenkirchen underwent a massive transformation and now focuses on solar energy and green technologies. The Veltins-Arena is home to the legendary football club FC Schalke 04, which forms the beating heart of local identity. Cultural highlights like the Musiktheater im Revier and the Nordsternpark, built on a former colliery site, round off the picture of the modern city.

Facts

  • Historically the undisputed stronghold of German hard coal mining ('City of 1000 Fires').
  • Home to FC Schalke 04, one of Germany's most famous and tradition-rich football clubs.
  • The Veltins-Arena is among Europe's most modern stadiums, featuring a retractable roof.
  • Today, it actively brands itself as a 'Solar City' and a hub for renewable energy tech.
  • The Musiktheater im Revier (MiR) is an outstanding example of post-war modern architecture.
  • The Nordsternpark was built on a former coal mine for the 1997 Federal Garden Show.
  • Schloss Berge is a highly impressive and historic moated castle within the city.
  • The ZOOM Erlebniswelt is an award-winning, heavily nature-oriented zoological park.

Aachen

Aachen is the westernmost major city in Germany, located in the border triangle with the Netherlands and Belgium. The historical significance of the city is enormous, as it was the preferred residence of Emperor Charlemagne, whose tomb is located in the impressive Aachen Cathedral. Due to its hot thermal springs, Aachen is a traditional spa town. The local specialty, the Aachener Printen (gingerbread), is popular far beyond the borders.

Bonn

Bonn is located in the south of North Rhine-Westphalia on the banks of the Rhine and has a history spanning over 2,000 years. The city is known worldwide as the birthplace of the famous composer Ludwig van Beethoven. From 1949 until the reunification of Germany, Bonn served as the seat of government and provisional capital of the Federal Republic. Today, the city distinguishes itself as a UN location, headquarters for many international organizations, and a center for science and telecommunications.

Duisburg

Duisburg is located at the confluence of the Ruhr and Rhine rivers and heavily shapes the western Ruhr area through its waterfront location. The city is home to the world's largest inland port, a crucial hub in European logistics. Once purely a steel and coal center, Duisburg is successfully transforming today through projects like the Landschaftspark Duisburg-Nord. The historical industrial culture harmoniously blends with new leisure and cultural offerings.

Oberhausen

Oberhausen is located in the western Ruhr Area in North Rhine-Westphalia and stands as a symbol of the region's successful structural transformation. Once known as the 'Cradle of the Ruhr Industry,' iron and steel production dominated here for decades. Today, the 'Neue Mitte Oberhausen' (New Center) is the heart of the city, built on former industrial wastelands. It is home to CentrO, one of Europe's largest shopping and leisure centers. The Gasometer, a monumental former gas holder, now serves as a gigantic exhibition hall and is the city's highly visible landmark.

Bochum

Bochum is a major city in the heart of the Ruhr Area, embodying the successful transition from heavy industry to a knowledge and service-based society. In the 1960s, the first university in the Ruhr region, the Ruhr University Bochum (RUB), was founded here, marking the dawn of a new era. Culturally, the city is nationally renowned for its award-winning theater, Schauspielhaus Bochum, and the musical 'Starlight Express,' which has been running for decades. The German Mining Museum offers fascinating insights into the industrial past, both below and above ground.
