Brandenburg Gate

View on the map

The Brandenburg Gate in Berlin is Germany's most famous landmark and a national symbol. It was built in the late 18th century as an early classical triumphal arch. During the Cold War, it stood in the exclusion zone of the Berlin Wall and was a symbol of division. With the fall of the Wall in 1989, it became the emotional emblem of German and European reunification.

Facts

  • Built between 1788 and 1791 according to designs by Carl Gotthard Langhans.
  • Crowned by the Quadriga, a chariot drawn by four horses.
  • The Quadriga was stolen by Napoleon in 1806 to Paris and later returned.
  • The scene of jubilant crowds during the fall of the Wall in 1989.
  • Located at Pariser Platz, at the end of the Unter den Linden boulevard.
  • The only surviving gate of the former 18 Berlin city gates.
  • Depicted on the German 10, 20, and 50 euro cent coins.
  • Often a central location for major events and New Year's Eve parties.
