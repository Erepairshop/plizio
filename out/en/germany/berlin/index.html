Cities

Berlin

Germany's capital and one of Europe's most significant cultural centers, with over 3.6 million inhabitants. The city blends history and modernity, from the Brandenburg Gate to the Spree River.

History

Berliner Mauer

The Berlin Wall was a 155 km fortification that separated West Berlin from East Berlin and East Germany from 1961 to 1989. It stood as a symbol of German division and the Cold War, and its fall on 9 November 1989 was a world-historic event.

Read more

Checkpoint Charlie

Checkpoint Charlie was the most famous border crossing between East and West Berlin during Germany's division. Dramatic scenes occurred here, including vehicle break-throughs and a tank confrontation between US and Soviet tanks in October 1961.

Read more

Stasi Museum

The Stasi Museum in Berlin is located in the former headquarters of the East German Ministry for State Security. It documents the surveillance and repression methods of the secret service, which was one of history's most effective and brutal secret police forces.

Read more

DDR Museum

The DDR Museum in Berlin presents the everyday life of the German Democratic Republic interactively and shows how people lived behind the Iron Curtain. Visitors can touch original GDR objects and get into a Trabant.

Read more

Reichstag 1945

The Reichstag building in Berlin at the end of World War II was one of the symbols of Nazi Germany's defeat, when Soviet soldiers hoisted the Red Flag on the destroyed building on 2 May 1945. This historic photograph became one of the most famous images of the war.

Read more

November Revolution 1918 – Reichstag

On 9 November 1918, Philipp Scheidemann proclaimed the German Republic from the window of the Reichstag building in Berlin, thereby ending the monarchy. The November Revolution led to the abdication of Kaiser Wilhelm II and the founding of the Weimar Republic.

Spartacist Uprising 1919 – Berlin

The Spartacist Uprising in January 1919 was an armed revolt by the Communist Party of Germany and the Spartacus League in Berlin, which was violently suppressed by the new republic. Rosa Luxemburg and Karl Liebknecht, the leaders of the uprising, were murdered on 15 January 1919.

Read more

Wannsee Conference – Memorial

On 20 January 1942, senior SS and government officials planned the systematic murder of European Jews in the villa at Wannsee in Berlin-Zehlendorf – the so-called 'Final Solution'. The villa is today a memorial and educational centre commemorating this crime.

Read more

Berlin Olympic Stadium 1936

The Olympic Stadium in Berlin-Charlottenburg was built for the 1936 Summer Olympics, which the Nazi regime used as a propaganda event. American sprinter Jesse Owens won four gold medals, publicly disproving the National Socialist racial ideology.

Landmarks

Brandenburg Gate

The Brandenburg Gate is Berlin's most famous landmark, built in the 18th century as a neoclassical city gate. During the German division it was a symbol of the Cold War and after reunification in 1990 became a symbol of German unity.

Read more

Reichstag Building

The Reichstag Building in Berlin is the seat of the German Bundestag and one of Germany's most significant political buildings. The historic building was completed in 1894 and after reunification was fitted with a modern glass dome by British architect Norman Foster.

Read more

Berlin TV Tower

The Berlin TV Tower stands at Alexanderplatz and at 368 m is Germany's tallest structure. It was built in 1969 in East Germany as a symbol of socialism and is today Berlin's most recognizable landmark.
