Wartburg

View on the map

Wartburg is a medieval castle in Thuringia near Eisenach, where Martin Luther translated the Bible into German in 1521–22 under the pseudonym 'Junker Jörg'. The castle is a symbol of German history and the Reformation and has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1999.

Facts

  • UNESCO World Heritage since 1999.
  • Luther translated the Bible here.
  • Symbol of the German Reformation.

Erfurt

Erfurt, the idyllic state capital of Thuringia, fascinates with one of the best-preserved medieval city centers in Germany. The city is famous for the Krämerbrücke, the longest bridge in Europe continuously built and inhabited with houses. As early as the Middle Ages, Erfurt grew rich from woad cultivation and trade. Martin Luther also spent formative years here as a student and Augustinian monk, making the city an important site of the Reformation.

Jena

Jena, located in the Saale valley in Thuringia, is a city globally renowned as a center for optics and precision engineering. In the 19th century, Carl Zeiss, Ernst Abbe, and Otto Schott laid the foundations for the modern optical industry here. This scientific and industrial heritage is also reflected in the Friedrich Schiller University, which dominates the city's dynamic life. Its striking landmark is the Jentower, a skyscraper that towers over the city like a giant telescope. Today, Jena successfully blends a rich scientific tradition with modern high-tech entrepreneurship.

Weimar

Weimar is a relatively small city in Thuringia, but it boasts an incomparable density of cultural and historical significance. As the center of Weimar Classicism, poets Johann Wolfgang von Goethe and Friedrich Schiller attracted intellectuals from all over Europe. Later, in 1919, the pioneering Bauhaus movement was founded here, and the first democratic constitution of Germany, which gave the Weimar Republic its name, was signed. This immense historical significance makes Weimar a unique cultural city.

KZ Buchenwald

Buchenwald concentration camp near Weimar in Thuringia was one of Germany's largest concentration camps in World War II, where over 56,000 people lost their lives. The Buchenwald Memorial commemorates the suffering of the victims and warns against inhumanity.

Wartburg

Wartburg Castle near Eisenach in Thuringia is one of Germany's most significant medieval fortresses, where Martin Luther translated the New Testament into German in 1521–22. The castle is also famous for the Wartburg Festival of 1817, where students publicly called for German unity and freedom for the first time.
