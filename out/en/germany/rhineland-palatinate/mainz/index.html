Mainz

View on the map

Mainz is the state capital of Rhineland-Palatinate and boasts over 2,000 years of history dating back to the Romans. The city on the Rhine is world-famous as the birthplace of Johannes Gutenberg, the inventor of the printing press with movable type, to whom a major museum is dedicated here. The old town is dominated by the imposing, over 1,000-year-old Mainz Cathedral. Mainz is also one of the most important strongholds of the Rhenish Carnival (Fastnacht), reflecting the cheerful and open character of the city. As a media hub, it houses the headquarters of the ZDF broadcasting network.

Facts

  • The state capital and the largest city in Rhineland-Palatinate.
  • Birthplace of Johannes Gutenberg, inventor of the modern printing press.
  • The Mainz Cathedral is one of the most important Romanesque cathedrals.
  • Considered one of the major strongholds of the traditional Rhenish Carnival.
  • The Romans founded the legionary camp Mogontiacum here.
  • As a prominent media city, it is the headquarters of the ZDF and SWR networks.
  • The Gutenberg Museum attracts typography and history enthusiasts globally.
  • Located directly across from Wiesbaden at the confluence of the Rhine and Main rivers.

Trier

Trier in Rhineland-Palatinate is proudly the oldest city in Germany, founded by the Romans around 16 BC. In antiquity, 'Augusta Treverorum' was temporarily the capital of the Western Roman Empire and had tens of thousands of inhabitants. Nowhere else in Germany are Roman structures so numerous and well-preserved, foremost the monumental Porta Nigra, the huge amphitheater, and the Imperial Baths. Today, Trier is also known as the birthplace of Karl Marx and as a picturesque wine city on the Moselle river.

Speyer Cathedral

Speyer Cathedral is Germany's most important Romanesque cathedral and one of the world's largest cathedrals, built between 1030 and 1061. It is the burial place of several German emperors and kings and has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1981.

Porta Nigra

The Porta Nigra is the best-preserved Roman city gate north of the Alps and is located in Trier. The 2nd-century structure is an impressive testimony to Roman urban architecture and has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1986.

Hambacher Schloss

Hambacher Schloss near Neustadt an der Weinstraße was in 1832 the venue of the Hambach Festival, the largest political demonstration of the German pre-revolutionary era with up to 30,000 participants. The festival is considered the birth of the German democracy movement.

Speyerer Dom

Speyer Cathedral is the largest preserved Romanesque church in the world and was built between 1030 and 1061 under Emperor Conrad II. It served as the imperial burial place of the Salian dynasty and has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1981.
