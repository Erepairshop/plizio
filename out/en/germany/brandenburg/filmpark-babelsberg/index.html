Filmpark Babelsberg

View on the map

Filmpark Babelsberg near Potsdam is the world's oldest major film studio, founded in 1912, and one of Europe's most important film production sites. Visitors can tour film sets and experience studio atmosphere.

Facts

  • Founded 1912 – world's oldest major film studio.
  • Metropolis (1927) and other classics were made here.
  • Today also a theme park with stunts and shows.

Sanssouci Palace

Sanssouci Palace in Potsdam is the former summer palace of Frederick the Great and a masterpiece of Prussian Rococo. The name means 'without worries' in French and the park with its terraces, fountains, and sculptures has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1990.

Potsdam

Brandenburg's capital lies southwest of Berlin and is best known for Sanssouci Palace and extensive parklands that are part of the UNESCO World Heritage. Potsdam was the residence city of the Prussian kings.

Spreewald

The Spreewald is a unique floodplain landscape in Brandenburg where the Spree divides into hundreds of small channels. The UNESCO Biosphere Reserve is home to the Sorbian minority and known for its punt ferries, traditional folk costumes, and world-famous Spreewald gherkins.

Cecilienhof Palace

Cecilienhof Palace in Potsdam was the venue of the Potsdam Conference in July and August 1945, where the Allies decided on the reorganization of Europe and Germany after World War II. The palace was completed in 1917 as the last residence of the Hohenzollerns.

Sachsenhausen Memorial

Sachsenhausen concentration camp near Oranienburg north of Berlin was from 1936 to 1945 a central camp of the Nazi regime, in which over 200,000 people were imprisoned and tens of thousands died. Today the site is a memorial and museum.

Spreewald – Gherkins

In the Spreewald in Brandenburg, gherkins have been grown for centuries and are world-famous as Spreewald gherkins. The gherkins are traditionally pickled in wooden barrels and transported by punt boat through the channels.
