Fläming

View on the map

The Fläming is a gentle hilly landscape in southwestern Brandenburg with extensive pine forests and agricultural land. The area is part of the Fläming Nature Park and offers numerous hiking and cycling routes. The region is known for its asparagus and apples.

The Fläming is a unique upland region in Brandenburg and Saxony-Anhalt, characterized by its rolling hills of glacial sand and its deep connection to the Flemish settlers of the 12th century. This 'land of the Flamings' is famous for its vast forests, traditional stone churches, and the world-unique 'Flaeming-Skate,' a 230-kilometer network of high-quality asphalt paths designed specifically for skaters and cyclists. The landscape is a mosaic of dense woods, clear streams, and quiet villages where time feels as if it has stood still. It is a haven for biodiversity, protecting rare orchids and bird species in its diverse nature parks. The Fläming remains a symbol of the quiet, rural soul of the German plains. Facts

  • The Fläming stretches from Brandenburg into Saxony-Anhalt.
  • The highest point is Hagelberg at 178 m.
  • The Hoher Fläming Nature Park protects the landscape.
  • Fläming asparagus is well known far beyond Brandenburg.
  • A glacial moraine landscape formed during the Saale glaciation.
  • Named after the Flemish settlers who arrived in the 12th century.
  • Home to the 'Flaeming-Skate', Europe's longest skating circuit.
  • Highest point is the Hagelberg at 200 meters altitude.
  • Famous for its numerous medieval 'fieldstone' churches (Feldsteinkirchen).
  • Includes the Hoher Fläming Nature Park, covering 82,700 hectares.

Related places

Spreewald

The Spreewald in Brandenburg is a unique and magical floodplain landscape created by the branching of the Spree River into hundreds of small canals. This ice-age-formed region is a UNESCO Biosphere Reserve. The Spreewald is home to the Sorbs, a Slavic minority whose culture, customs, and bilingual signs characterize the area to this day. The region is famous for romantic trips in traditional Spreewald punts and for the crisp Spreewald gherkins.

Potsdam

The Brandenburg state capital Potsdam is a city of kings and cinema, idyllically surrounded by water and forest. It is famous for the magnificent palace and park complexes of Sanssouci, built by Frederick the Great, which are now a UNESCO World Heritage site. In addition, world history was written in Potsdam in 1945 at Cecilienhof Palace, when the victorious powers met for the Potsdam Conference. The historical Babelsberg film studio also makes the city a significant European cultural metropolis.

Sanssouci Palace

Sanssouci Palace in Potsdam is the former summer palace of Frederick the Great and a masterpiece of Prussian Rococo. The name means 'without worries' in French and the park with its terraces, fountains, and sculptures has been a UNESCO World Heritage Site since 1990.

Cecilienhof Palace

Cecilienhof Palace in Potsdam was the venue of the Potsdam Conference in July and August 1945, where the Allies decided on the reorganization of Europe and Germany after World War II. The palace was completed in 1917 as the last residence of the Hohenzollerns.

Sachsenhausen Memorial

Sachsenhausen concentration camp near Oranienburg north of Berlin was from 1936 to 1945 a central camp of the Nazi regime, in which over 200,000 people were imprisoned and tens of thousands died. Today the site is a memorial and museum.

Spreewald – Gherkins

In the Spreewald in Brandenburg, gherkins have been grown for centuries and are world-famous as Spreewald gherkins. The gherkins are traditionally pickled in wooden barrels and transported by punt boat through the channels.
