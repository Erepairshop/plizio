Hannover

View on the map

Lower Saxony's capital is an important transport hub and trade fair city in northern Germany. Hannover hosted the World Exposition EXPO 2000 and boasts the magnificent Herrenhausen Gardens.

Facts

  • EXPO 2000 host city.
  • Herrenhausen Gardens.
  • Major trade fair city.

Braunschweig

Braunschweig is one of Lower Saxony's largest cities and was the residence of the Welf dynasty, one of the most important princely families of the Middle Ages. The city is known for Henry the Lion, its lion fountain, and as the technical center of the region.

Göttingen

Göttingen is a classic university town in Lower Saxony home to one of Germany's most prestigious universities, world-famous since the 18th century. More than 40 Nobel Prize winners have worked or studied here.

Lüneburger Heide

The Lüneburg Heath is a wide heathland and sandy landscape in Lower Saxony between Hamburg and Hannover, especially beautiful in late summer when the purple heather plants bloom. The nature reserve with its old heath villages and small Heidschnucke sheep flocks is unique in Central Europe.

Wolfenbüttel

Wolfenbüttel is a small city in Lower Saxony with one of northern Germany's most significant Baroque town centers. The Herzog August Library houses one of the most valuable medieval book collections and was once the world's largest library.

Altes Land – Apple Orchards

The Alte Land southwest of Hamburg is the largest connected fruit-growing area north of the Alps. Every spring the landscape transforms into a sea of apple blossoms.

Lüneburg Heath – Beekeeping

The Lüneburg Heath is famous for its heather honey, collected by bees from the blooming heather fields. Nomadic beekeepers travel through the heath with their hives when the purple heather blooms.
