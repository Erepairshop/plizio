Autostadt Wolfsburg

View on the map

Autostadt at the Mittelland Canal in Wolfsburg is Volkswagen's brand pavilion park with exhibitions of all VW Group brands, the Zeithaus and auto museum. New vehicles are handed over to customers here.

Facts

  • Annually: over 2 million visitors.
  • VW buyers collect their vehicles here.
  • Auto museum: over 200 historic vehicles.

Hannover

Hanover is the state capital of Lower Saxony and a major international trade fair city. Known as a green metropolis, it is home to the Eilenriede city forest and the famous Herrenhausen Gardens, which are masterpieces of garden art. Historically, Hanover was the residence city of the House of Welf and closely connected to the British royal family. Today, the city is an important hub for industry and technology.

Braunschweig

Braunschweig (Brunswick) is the second-largest city in Lower Saxony and looks back on a powerful history. In the Middle Ages, Henry the Lion developed it into an influential metropolis and an important member of the Hanseatic League. The bronze Welf monument, the Brunswick Lion on the Burgplatz, still testifies to this era today. The city was heavily damaged during World War II but managed to preserve parts of its historical heritage, such as the Cathedral. Today, Braunschweig is an excellent research city and a dynamic location for the European automotive and transportation industries.

Göttingen

Göttingen, located in southern Lower Saxony, is a classic German university city. Its centerpiece is the Georg-August University, founded in 1737, which is globally renowned for its academic excellence. The history of the city is inseparably linked with the more than 40 Nobel laureates who studied, researched, or taught here. The historic city center is surrounded by a well-preserved medieval rampart and captivates visitors with its picturesque half-timbered architecture. Göttingen's most famous landmark is the Gänseliesel, a fountain figure traditionally kissed by newly graduated doctoral students.

Lüneburger Heide

The Lüneburg Heath is a wide heathland and sandy landscape in Lower Saxony between Hamburg and Hannover, especially beautiful in late summer when the purple heather plants bloom. The nature reserve with its old heath villages and small Heidschnucke sheep flocks is unique in Central Europe.
